Quick Start

From import to a working map in your own scene. The Atlas Tour sample is the fastest way to see every feature first (guide: Sample Guide — Atlas Tour).

1. Run Setup (one click)

Open Tools > ByteLura > MiniAtlas Pro and click Run Setup. It creates Assets/ByteLura/MiniAtlas Pro/ in your project:

  • a map settings asset with five starter categories (Player, Character, Point of Interest, Objective, Marker),
  • a starter grid background and starter icon sprites, generated for you — replace any of them with your own art whenever you like.

Setup never touches your open scene, and re-running it never overwrites anything. It only fills in what is missing, and reports one plain-language line per item.

2. See it working (the Atlas Tour)

Click Open the Atlas Tour in the same window, then press Play:

  • Walk with WASD — fog lifts around the capsule.
  • The corner minimap tracks the player; the objective sits on its edge as an arrow until it comes into view.
  • Press M — the world map shows the explored shape of your walk, and nothing you have not explored.
  • Walk onto the objective in the far corner — the tour completes, and the hidden cache is revealed on both maps.

First success takes about two minutes. Everything the tour does, your game does through the same public API.

3. Put the map in your scene

  1. In the window's Map Setup section, click Add MiniAtlas Map to Scene.
  2. Select your ground object, then click Fit Bounds to Selected. The map bounds now match your world exactly — this is the calibration step, and it is the only measurement the kit ever asks for.
  3. Assign your background art in the settings. Draw it as a top-down picture of your world inside the bounds. The window checks the aspect for you: if the image aspect and the bounds aspect disagree, the map would stretch, and the window says so in plain words.

No background art yet? The starter grid background stands in — the map works fully while your art is being made.

4. Mark your world

Add a Map Marker component (Add Component > ByteLura > MiniAtlas Pro > Map Marker) to anything that belongs on the map:

  • Category decides the icon, tint, and which views show it.
  • Visibility rule decides when fog hides it: When Explored for places, When Visible for creatures, Always for the player, Manual for secrets your code reveals.
  • On your player, also tick Reveals Fog and set the reveal radius.

5. Add the views

  • Minimap: a MinimapView component on a UI panel; set its Follow Target to your player. Full details: Minimap View.
  • World map: a WorldMapView component on a UI object in your canvas; it builds its own panel and opens on M. Details: World Map View.

6. Check the install

Click Verify Install in the window (or Tools > ByteLura > MiniAtlas Pro > Verify Install). Five checks in plain language: runtime assembly, an in-editor map self-test (projection center, fog reveal, fog save/load round-trip), settings present, sample present, documentation present. If a line fails, its wording is the diagnosis.
